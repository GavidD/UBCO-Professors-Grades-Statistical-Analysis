How grading differs between professors teaching the same course

gradegap compares each professor's grade averages with those of other professors who taught the same courses at UBC. It is a statistics project, and a way to see how much the instructor can matter when you plan your courses.

/

How to read the table

Grades vs colleagues (Diff) is the professor's average minus everyone else's average in the same courses, in percentage points, weighted by how many of the professor's students were in each course. The bar shows that number and the thin line is its 95% confidence interval. A clear gap means the interval excludes zero. Rows without one are drawn hollow and in grey.

Worked example (fictional). A professor taught Course A (120 students, average 74) and Course B (40 students, average 70). Colleagues averaged 71 in A and 68 in B, so the course differences are +3 and +2. Weighted by students: (3 × 120 + 2 × 40) ÷ 160 = +2.75 points.

With hundreds of professors, a handful will show a clear gap by chance alone. Treat extremes as leads, not verdicts.

About

I'm David Goerzen, a physics student at the University of British Columbia's Okanagan campus. I created gradegap to examine how grading varies between professors teaching the same courses, and to understand whether the choice of instructor can meaningfully affect a student's GPA.

The code is open source under the MIT licence on GitHub. Found a mistake? Open an issue.

Grade data comes from UBCGrades, built by DonneyF. Thank you.
